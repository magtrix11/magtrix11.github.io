# Visual Rules Derived from the Completed Mood Board

## 1. Overall character

The visual world is rich, tactile, biological, archaeological, bodily, and strange. It combines painting, fiber, soft sculpture, hand-built objects, manuscript-like notation, impossible botany, masks/faces, vessels, and suspended or enclosed bodies. It is neither clean minimalism nor visual chaos. Density and emptiness should alternate like contraction and breath.

## 2. Color

Use the board's actual chromatic intensity. Strong candidates include saturated cobalt and ultramarine, arterial red, coral and translucent pink, yellow and ochre, leaf and acid greens, plum, violet, rust, and orange. Bone, cream, charcoal, earth, metal, and black can provide structure, but they must not flatten the work into a beige or neutral craft aesthetic.

Color should feel embedded in matter: stained, painted, layered, woven, oxidized, translucent, rubbed away, or glowing through a membrane. Avoid digital rainbow gradients unless their colors have a material source in the frame.

## 3. Form vocabulary

- Elongated soft bodies and hanging or folded forms.
- Fiber organisms, knots, roots, vessels, nerves, tendrils, cords, and vascular tubes.
- Fragmented faces, masks, closed eyes, mouths, and partial bodily recognition.
- Layered fragments, nested skins, cavities, enclosures, cages, armatures, and cocoons.
- Ribs, membranes, fins, leaves, and wing-like structures that remain ambiguous.
- Impossible botanical or anatomical systems with manuscript-like intelligence.
- Gold or metallic anthropomorphic structures used for lessons in compression, articulation, suspension, and transformed bodies—not as copied ornament.
- Painted shards and woven planes that can change function while retaining identity.

Avoid perfect bilateral symmetry. Even when a form is frontal or iconic, preserve handmade imbalance and unresolved edges.

## 4. Surface and light

Prioritize visibly specific surfaces: scanned paint, worn canvas, woven fibers, frayed edges, translucent cloth, thread tension, rough paper, metal, pearl, ceramic, cast shadows, stains, seams, and accumulated pigment. Depth should often come from layering and occlusion rather than generic 3D lighting.

Use translucency as bodily evidence: light through tissue, cloth, resin, skin, or wing membrane. Shadows may reveal unseen structure. Retain grain, imperfect registration, abrasions, and small discontinuities.

## 5. Motion logic

Motion is reorganization, not replacement. A form may fold, gather, split, knot, invert, stretch, weave, molt, compress, suspend, or reveal an interior, but its previous matter remains traceable. Favor persistence, accumulation, tension, drag, friction, and delayed response over weightless morphing.

The work may stutter, step, pulse, or retain afterimages. These digital qualities should converse with stop-motion, weaving, scanning, and hand manipulation rather than simulate seamless cinematic realism.

## 6. Digital behavior

Visible digitalness is desired. It can appear through:

- Generative thread/path networks with memory and tension.
- Mesh deformation and vertex displacement anchored to material textures.
- Frame stepping, held frames, small discontinuities, and intentional registration shifts.
- Feedback buffers, afterimages, persistence, and traces of earlier states.
- Scan lines, pixel sorting in restrained regions, dithering, chromatic misregistration, thresholding, or compression scars.
- Procedural growth guided by drawn masks, painted contours, or fiber direction.
- Data-like notation and path systems that behave like anatomy or botany.
- Layered 2D/2.5D surfaces rather than glossy default 3D objects.

Do not use an effect because it reads as “digital.” Every behavior must describe memory, continuity, bodily change, material stress, or conceal/reveal.

## 7. Cultural and historical responsibility

Colombian and Antioquian references should be structurally meaningful and carefully researched. Learn from material logic, transformation, concealment, suspension, bodily compression, surface, movement, and relationships between object and ritual space. Do not fabricate generalized Indigenous patterns or copy a historical object as visual decoration. Record sources for any direct historical research added later.

## 8. Hierarchy of authority

1. The project's ethical and conceptual thesis in `TREATMENT.md`.
2. The formal evidence in the supplied mood board.
3. Toni's original artwork and materials added to `assets/artwork/`.
4. Technical choices that serve those sources.

Software defaults never outrank the sources.
