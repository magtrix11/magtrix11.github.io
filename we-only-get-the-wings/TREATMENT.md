**WE ONLY GET THE WINGS**

*Working Visual Treatment + AI Previsualization Guide*

Experimental stop-motion / moving-painting collaboration about
continuity through bodily transformation.

**Core proposition: metamorphosis without disappearance.**

# 1. Core Thesis

This is not a story of death and rebirth. It is a story of continuity
through radical transformation.

The larva does not die so the butterfly can exist. The organism is
continuous even when its form becomes almost impossible to recognize.
The visual language of the film should therefore reject a clean “before”
and “after.” Earlier materials must remain inside later states.

A scrap of painted canvas introduced early can later become part of an
eyelid, then disappear from view, then return as a rib inside a
wing-like structure. One thread can physically survive the entire film.
The viewer receives only evidence that the earlier form is still
present: a seam, a pearl, a fragment of pigment, a mask, a wing, a
repeated curve.

The governing idea is: Nothing disappears. It becomes difficult to
recognize.

**Central phrase: “We only get the wings.”**

# 2. What the Film Should Feel Like

- Handmade, tactile, materially specific, slightly uncanny.

- Like a moving manuscript documenting a species of consciousness that
  does not have a name yet.

- Somewhere between stop-motion sculpture, archival specimen
  photography, cipher manuscript, experimental film, painting,
  archaeological object, and ritual presence.

- Ancient and futuristic at the same time, without becoming “mystical”
  in a generic way.

- The hand should remain visible: loose threads, rough cuts, puckered
  cloth, paint cracks, uneven seams, cast shadows, imperfect symmetry.

## Avoid

- Inspirational-disability clichés or “overcoming SCI” as a neat triumph
  narrative.

- Literal hospital, crash, wheelchair, or accident reenactment imagery.

- Generic floral decoration, cottagecore, pressed-flower craft
  aesthetics.

- Literal butterflies or rainbow metamorphosis imagery.

- Generic “tribal” styling, pseudo-Indigenous motifs, or copied
  sacred/historical objects.

- Bohemian craft styling, dreamcatcher language, psychedelic neon,
  glossy CGI perfection.

# 3. Colombian / Antioquian Research Direction

The Colombian layer should be specific, researched, and structural
rather than decorative. “Pre-Columbian” should not function as one
flattened aesthetic category. Use individual objects and regional
material traditions as formal research, and keep provenance attached to
the reference.

## Primary research lanes

- Antioquia / ancestral-land lane: archaeological ceramics, incised
  surfaces, material memory, objects from Antioquia and the Valle de
  Aburrá, especially research through the Museo Universitario de la
  Universidad de Antioquia.

- Colombian face / mask / ornament lane: selected Quimbaya and
  Calima/Yotoco objects in which the face is obscured, multiplied,
  adorned, extended, or transformed through material and motion.

- Voynich lane: not “flowers,” but impossible botany —
  root/nerve/vein/plant forms that look taxonomic yet cannot be
  classified.

## Rule for cultural references

When saving or using a historical object, record: culture or region;
approximate date; museum/source; and the exact formal property being
studied. Example: “Calima (Yotoco), Colombia — interested in radial
extension, suspended moving elements, reflected light, and face embedded
inside a larger structure.”

Do not reproduce an existing mask, ornament, glyph, or Indigenous motif.
Translate structural principles: frontality, layered conceal/reveal,
incision, suspension, radiating extension, asymmetry, repeated hanging
elements, and movement activated by the body.

## Research anchors

- Museo Universitario, Universidad de Antioquia — archaeology /
  anthropology collections.

- Museo de Antioquia — Quimbaya anthropomorphic mask and regional
  collections.

- The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Calima/Yotoco pectorals, masks,
  pendants, and headdress ornaments.

- Yale Beinecke Library — Voynich Manuscript botanical and diagrammatic
  pages.

# 4. Visual System

## Four visual worlds

**A. The Table / Archive** — Warm neutral surface. Sparse fragments.
Canvas, fibers, thread, pearls, painted scraps, metal pieces. Objects
feel catalogued but unlabeled. Mostly overhead camera. Negative space.

**B. The Organism** — The archive begins producing life. Thread
migrates. Fabric curls. Pearls behave like seeds, cells, eggs, joints,
or eyes. Canvas pieces pulse, coil, and cluster. Scale becomes
uncertain.

**C. The Mask** — The organism develops a face, but never a simple
portrait. Eyes appear before the full face. Cheek becomes landscape.
Mouth disappears under cloth. The mask is another form of the face, not
a false face hiding a true one.

**D. The Wing** — Expansion through folding, bilateral suggestion,
membrane, ribbing, suspension, radiating pieces, and flutter. It should
be readable as wing / rib cage / artifact / page / appendage all at
once, never a literal butterfly.

## Color system

- Base: bone, parchment, raw canvas, warm off-white.

- Shadow: charcoal, almost-black, graphite.

- Earth: clay brown, umber, oxidized surfaces.

- Metal: dull, aged, hammered gold — reflective only in brief flashes.

- Living intrusions: small concentrated fragments of color already
  present in the paintings — e.g. one red, acid green, cobalt, pink —
  used as biological events rather than a broad palette.

## Material bible

- Cut painted canvas from the practice; raw canvas; muslin; thin linen.

- Black thread; embroidery floss; loose fiber; yarn in restrained
  amounts.

- Pearls or pearl-like beads; tiny metal discs; thin wire hidden in the
  build.

- Handmade paper, tracing paper, vellum, paper pulp.

- Paint skins, dried acrylic fragments, oil pastel marks, charcoal,
  graphite.

- Clay or ceramic-like fragments; wax; sparse metallic foil or gold
  leaf.

- Optional: fabric stiffened with starch or matte medium; translucent
  membranes.

## Recurring symbol grammar

| Symbol        | First reading  | Later reading                            |
|---------------|----------------|------------------------------------------|
| Thread        | continuity     | nerve / lineage / route / tether         |
| Pearl / seed  | latent form    | eye / cell / memory / joint              |
| Eyelid        | inwardness     | passage / surrender / recognition        |
| Mask          | altered face   | multiplicity / ancestor / new embodiment |
| Incision      | wound / mark   | writing / map / inheritance              |
| Fold          | concealment    | cocoon / body / wing                     |
| Wing          | transformation | expanded form, not escape                |
| Fragment      | apparent loss  | proof of continuity                      |
| Spiral / coil | larval body    | movement / becoming                      |
| Empty cutout  | absence        | another kind of presence                 |

**4. Wink I \| 0:28–0:35**

## Movement 2 — Body Before Language

**5. False Botany \| 0:35–0:48**

Fragments connect into a root / limb / nervous-system branch that

**6. Incision \| 0:48–0:59**

A line appears on cloth frame by frame: stitch / cut / graphite /

**7. First Eye \| 0:59–1:07**

The incision curves and becomes readable as an eye. No full face. The

## Movement 3 — Rupture

**8. Dislocation \| 1:07–1:17**

Archive begins separating in incompatible directions. Thread stretches
between fragments.

**9. Limit \| 1:17–1:25**

Thread reaches full tension. Everything stops. Tiny vibration. This is
the closest the film gets to the accident.

**10. Break Without Disappearance \| 1:25–1:32**

Thread does not snap. It loops and changes direction, creating a new
route.

## Movement 4 — Larva

**11. Condensation \| 1:32–1:45**

Fragments migrate inward. Pearls gather. Threads coil. Fabric wrinkles
around them into a soft unfamiliar organism.

**12. Pulse \| 1:45–1:55**

Organism expands and contracts through stop motion, loosely related to
bass but keeping its own timing.

**13. Multiplication \| 1:55–2:05**

One organism becomes three, or three are revealed. The same painted
fragment repeats through match cuts.

**14. Internal Landscape \| 2:05–2:17**

Extreme macro travel across folds. Pearl becomes moon, thread becomes
horizon, paint becomes terrain. Scale collapses.

## Movement 5 — Mask / Self

**15. Face Attempt I \| 2:17–2:27**

Fragments form an approximate face — asymmetrical, incomplete, almost
looking back — then collapse.

**16. Face Attempt II \| 2:27–2:38**

A new face assembles. Eyes become legible. Layered material conceals
parts of it. Borrow frontality and conceal/reveal, not a historical
design.

**17. Obscured Face \| 2:38–2:48**

A textile ornament-like element crosses the lower face. Only the eyes
remain visible.

**18. Eyes Close \| 2:48–2:58**

Hero image. Fabric eyelids close slowly. Nothing else moves. Hold after
closure. Not death: interior movement.

## Movement 6 — Inside

**19. Black Field / Thread \| 2:58–3:08**

Darkness. The same thread from early in the film slowly enters frame.

**20. Code \| 3:08–3:20**

Thread creates invented notation: stitch / anatomy / handwriting /
breathing / repetitive movement. Do not copy Voynich glyphs.

## Movement 7 — Expansion

**21. First Extension \| 3:20–3:29**

One side unfolds. It almost reads as a wing, then folds back. Refuse the
payoff.

**22. Wing Without Butterfly \| 3:29–3:40**

Mirrored extension: fabric ribs, hanging thread, dull metal, painted
membrane. Wing / rib cage / artifact / anatomical diagram.

**23. Motion Makes Object \| 3:40–3:52**

Suspended elements move with bass or air. The object changes because it
moves.

**24. Light \| 3:52–4:02**

Lighting angle changes. Small metallic pieces flash briefly. The
organism “winks” through reflected light.

## Movement 8 — We Only Get the Wings

**25. Reassembly \| 4:02–4:15**

Earlier objects return: first painted fragment, pearl, thread, incision,
eyelid. Each now serves a new function.

**26. Full Form \| 4:15–4:27**

Longest reveal. Complete organism: bilateral-ish, imperfect, face hidden
inside a larger impossible structure.

**27. Face / Wing Match Cut \| 4:27–4:35**

Macro eye → wing seam → closed eyelid → folded textile. Anatomy and
object become interchangeable.

**28. Final Wink \| 4:35–4:43**

Full organism. One tiny section moves or a pearl catches light.

**29. Withdrawal \| 4:43–4:52**

Wings do not fly away. They slowly close inward.

**30. Thread \| 4:52–end**

Black. Single thread or one fragment remains. Voice finishes. No
explanation.

# 6. Camera, Motion, and Lighting Rules

## Camera modes

**God / Archive** — Perfect overhead. Flat and still. Objects become
symbols. Use for scattering, cataloguing, assembly.

**Body / Macro** — Very close, shallow focus. Fabric becomes flesh or
landscape. Use for eye, stitch, thread, pearl, folds.

**Presence / Frontal** — Object faces camera directly and becomes
monumental. Use rarely, only when the form “looks back.”

## Motion grammar

- Thread: snakes, pulls, draws, connects.

- Pearls: roll, cluster, orbit.

- Fabric: breathes, unfolds, collapses, puckers.

- Canvas scraps: migrate, rotate, assemble.

- Mask: largely still.

- Wings: unfold, tremble, suspend.

## Lighting

- Begin with soft diffuse side light and warm-neutral white balance.

- During rupture, let shadows become harder and longer.

- Larval section: low-angle light that exaggerates topography.

- Mask: restrained frontal or slightly high light so texture stays
  visible.

- Wing sequence: moving grazing light so metallic details appear and
  disappear.

- Avoid neon LEDs, generic purple music-video light, smoke-machine
  mysticism, and broad rainbow lighting.

# 13. Short Treatment Copy

WE ONLY GET THE WINGS is a tactile stop-motion visual work exploring
bodily transformation after spinal cord injury without using the
familiar narrative of death and rebirth. Fabric, fragments of paintings,
thread, pearls, handmade forms, mask-like faces and wing structures
continually disassemble and return in altered configurations. The work
treats metamorphosis as continuity: the organism does not die when its
shape becomes unrecognizable.

Its visual language grows from Mariantonia Gutierrez’s painting
practice, impossible botanical and anatomical systems reminiscent of
cipher manuscripts, archaeological material from Antioquia, and
carefully researched pre-Hispanic Colombian objects in which faces are
layered, concealed, adorned and transformed. The result is less a
depiction of recovery than a moving artifact — a record of a body and
self continually becoming.

# 14. Collaborator Summary

You bring the sound. I build the organism.

The visualizer is constructed through stop-motion using fabric, cut
canvas, thread, pearls, fragments of old and new paintings, and handmade
objects. Conceptually, it is metamorphosis without disappearance: the
self remains continuous even when the form becomes almost
unrecognizable. We only receive glimpses of that continuity — an eye, a
mask, a repeated thread, a seam becoming a wing. Colombian and
Antioquian research enters through material logic, archaeological
surfaces, conceal/reveal, movement, and carefully sourced historical
references rather than copied motifs.
